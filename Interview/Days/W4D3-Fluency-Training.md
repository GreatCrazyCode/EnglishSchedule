## Today's Mindset

> "Fluency is more important than accuracy in an interview."

The interviewer cares about:
- Can you communicate clearly? YES
- Can you keep a conversation going? YES
- Is your grammar 100% perfect? NOT REALLY

So today: NO stopping. NO restarting. Just keep talking.

---

## Filler Phrases (Buying Time Naturally)

When you need a moment to think, DON'T just go silent. Use these:

| Phrase | When to Use | Example |
|--------|-------------|---------|
| That's a great question... | When you need time | "That's a great question. Let me think..." |
| Let me think about that for a moment. | When you're stuck | "Let me think about that for a moment." |
| What I mean is... | When you said it wrong | "Sorry, what I mean is..." |
| To put it simply... | When your answer is getting complex | "To put it simply, I enjoy solving problems." |
| How should I put this... | When searching for words | "How should I put this... I really enjoy the challenge." |
| So basically... | When summarizing | "So basically, I led the frontend team." |

### Practice These Now
Say each phrase 3 times out loud. They need to feel automatic.

## Connector Words (Making Sentences Flow)

Use these to connect your ideas smoothly:

### Adding Information
- **Also** / **Plus** / **On top of that**
  - "I led the team. Plus, I also handled code reviews."
- **In addition** / **What's more**
  - "I know React. In addition, I've worked with Vue."

### Giving Examples
- **For example** / **For instance**
  - "I'm a fast learner. For example, I picked up Three.js in a month."
- **Like** (casual) / **Such as** (formal)
  - "I use modern tools, like React and TypeScript."

### Contrasting
- **However** / **That said** / **On the other hand**
  - "It was tough. However, we managed to deliver on time."
- **While** / **Although**
  - "While I was disappointed, I learned a lot from it."

### Sequencing
- **First... Then... Finally...**
  - "First, I assessed the situation. Then I made a plan. Finally, I executed it."
- **After that** / **Next** / **Eventually**
  - "I developed the tool. After that, the whole team started using it."

### Concluding
- **So** / **Overall** / **In the end**
  - "Overall, I think I'm a great fit for this role."
- **To sum up** / **All in all**
  - "To sum up, my experience and skills match your needs."

## Speed Drills

### Drill 1: 60-Second Talk (15 min)

Pick a topic. Talk for exactly 60 seconds WITHOUT stopping. If you run out of things to say, repeat something or add "another thing is..."

**Topics:**
1. My job (what you do every day)
2. A project I'm proud of
3. Why I like frontend development
4. What I've been learning recently
5. My ideal workplace

**Rules:**
- Set a 60-second timer
- NO pausing longer than 2 seconds
- NO switching to Chinese
- It's OK to make grammar mistakes
- Record every attempt

---

### Drill 2: Question Rapid Fire (15 min)

Answer each question in 30 seconds or less. Don't think too long - just start talking.

1. What do you do?
2. Why are you looking for a new job?
3. What's your strength?
4. Tell me about a project.
5. How do you handle stress?
6. Why our company?
7. Salary expectations?
8. Where do you see yourself in 5 years?
9. How do you handle conflict?
10. Any questions for us?

**After all 10:** Listen back. Where did you pause the longest? Those are your weak spots.

---

### Drill 3: Rephrase Challenge (10 min)

For each sentence, say it 3 different ways:

**"I have 10 years of frontend experience."**
1. "I've been doing frontend work for over a decade."
2. "I've spent the last 10 years building web applications."
3. "Frontend development has been my focus for 10+ years."

**"I led a team of 9 people."**
1. ___________
2. ___________
3. ___________

**"I want to work at your company."**
1. ___________
2. ___________
3. ___________

**"I can solve problems well."**
1. ___________
2. ___________
3. ___________

This exercise builds flexibility - so you never get stuck on one way of saying things.

---

### Drill 4: Shadow Speaking (10 min)

1. Listen to this reference answer (read it out loud as if someone else is saying it):

> "Hi, I'm a frontend developer with over 10 years of experience. Currently, I lead a team of 9 developers. My main responsibilities include task assignment, code reviews, and solving technical challenges. I mainly work with React and Vue. One thing I'm proud of is developing an automation tool that streamlined our team's workflow. I'm now looking for a new challenge where I can keep growing, and I think this role is a great fit."

2. Now try to say the same content IN YOUR OWN WORDS without looking at the text.

3. Compare: Where did you get stuck? What words couldn't you remember?

## Emergency Rescue Phrases

If you completely blank out during a real interview:

| Situation | What to Say |
|-----------|-------------|
| Forgot what they asked | "Sorry, could you repeat the question?" |
| Don't know the answer | "That's a great question. I haven't thought about it exactly that way, but..." |
| Need time | "Let me take a moment to think about that." |
| Made a grammar mistake | "Sorry, what I meant to say was..." |
| Don't understand a word | "I'm sorry, could you explain what you mean by [word]?" |
| Going off track | "But to answer your question more directly..." |

### Practice: Say each of these 3 times until they feel natural.

## Fluency Checklist

After today's practice, check:

- [ ] I can talk for 60 seconds on any topic without stopping
- [ ] I know at least 3 filler phrases and can use them naturally
- [ ] I can use First/Then/Finally to structure my answers
- [ ] I can rephrase my key answers in different ways
- [ ] I have 3-4 emergency phrases memorized
- [ ] I recorded myself and listened back at least once
