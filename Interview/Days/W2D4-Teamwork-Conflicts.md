## Useful Expressions

### Describing Team Friction (use diplomatic language!)
- **We had a difference in opinion about [what].**
  - We had a difference in opinion about how to structure the code.
- **There was a misunderstanding between us.**
  - There was a misunderstanding between us about priorities.
- **We didn't see eye to eye on [what].** (idiom: disagree)
  - We didn't see eye to eye on the technical approach.

**Words to AVOID:** annoying, bad, stupid, lazy, wrong (never badmouth others!)

### Describing Your Response
- **I tried to understand their perspective.**
  - Before reacting, I tried to understand their perspective.
- **I had a one-on-one conversation with them.**
  - I had a one-on-one conversation to clear things up.
- **I suggested [solution].**
  - I suggested pair programming so we could learn from each other.
- **I focused on [what] rather than [what].**
  - I focused on solving the problem rather than blaming anyone.

### Describing Results
- **After that, our teamwork improved a lot.**
- **We found a solution that worked for both of us.**
- **This experience taught me the importance of [what].**
  - This experience taught me the importance of open communication.

### General Conflict Handling
- **When conflicts arise, I [method].**
  - When conflicts arise, I first try to listen.
- **I believe the key is [what].**
  - I believe the key is communication and understanding.
- **My approach is to [method].**
  - My approach is to find common ground and move forward.

## Answer Framework

### Team Friction Story: Situation -> Your Method -> Result

```
Situation (1-2 sentences, stay neutral)
  -> "I had a colleague who [objective description, no judgment]."
  -> "We had different working styles."

Your Method (2-3 sentences, show communication)
  -> "I took the time to understand their perspective."
  -> "I suggested [specific solution] to help."

Result (1 sentence, positive)
  -> "After that, we worked together much better."
```

### Conflict Handling: 4-Step Method

```
Step 1: Listen
  -> "I first listen to understand the other person's point of view."

Step 2: Communicate
  -> "Then I share my thoughts calmly and honestly."

Step 3: Find a solution
  -> "We try to find a compromise that works for both sides."

Step 4: Move forward
  -> "I focus on the problem, not the person."
```

## Reference Answers (with Annotations)

### Q: Describe a time when you had to work with a difficult team member

> In my previous role, there was a team member who was **new to the team** and was still **getting up to speed**. This sometimes caused **delays** because they needed more time to finish their tasks.
>
> Instead of getting frustrated, I **tried to understand** their situation. They were dealing with an unfamiliar codebase, which is **completely normal** for a new person.
>
> So I **offered to do pair programming** with them a few times a week. I'd walk them through the code, explain the architecture, and answer their questions. This helped them **get up to speed** much faster.
>
> **After a few weeks**, their productivity improved a lot, and our **teamwork** got much better. I actually **learned a lot** from the experience too -- sometimes a little patience and support can make a **huge difference**.

**Notes:**
- Never said the person was "difficult" -- used "new to the team" and "getting up to speed" instead
- "instead of getting frustrated" -- shows emotional maturity
- "completely normal" -- shows understanding
- "offered to" = volunteered, shows initiative
- "huge difference" = big change, conversational

### Q: How do you handle conflicts with your colleagues?

> When I have a conflict with a colleague, I try to follow a few steps.
>
> **First**, I **listen carefully** to understand their **point of view**. Sometimes what seems like a conflict is actually just a **misunderstanding**.
>
> **Second**, I **share my thoughts** calmly and honestly. I focus on the **issue**, not the person. I try to say "I think we could try this approach" rather than "You're doing it wrong."
>
> **Third**, I look for a **compromise** -- a solution that works for **both of us**. Most of the time, people are reasonable when they feel **heard** and **respected**.
>
> I believe good teamwork comes from **open communication** and **mutual respect**. A little conflict is normal -- what matters is how you **handle** it.

**Notes:**
- "point of view" = opinion, more formal
- "focus on the issue, not the person" -- classic interview expression
- "heard and respected" -- very strong expression
- "mutual respect" = respect each other

## Common Mistakes

| Wrong | Problem | Correct |
|-------|---------|---------|
| He is a difficult person. | Too negative | We had **different working styles**. |
| I told him he was wrong. | Too confrontational | I **shared my perspective** and **listened** to his. |
| We fighted about it. | Wrong past tense | We **had a disagreement** about it. |
| I handle conflicts good. | good is adjective | I handle conflicts **well**. |
| We found a comprise. | Spelling | We found a **compromise**. |
| I try to understanding them. | Wrong verb form | I try to **understand** them. |
